01How we ranked them

The ranking uses market capitalisation (share price × shares in issue), which shows the size of a company. We multiplied the Tokyo Stock Exchange closing price[1] by the number of shares in issue (as at 2026-06-30, excluding treasury shares) in each company's first-quarter earnings summary (kessan tanshin) published in July–August 2026.[2] Won amounts use the Bank of Korea exchange rate.[3]

Only companies headquartered in Japan are included. Banks include listed financial holding companies that own banks, and for brokers we counted only listed companies that offer securities accounts and dealing to individual investors. The exchange operator (Japan Exchange Group) and bank-owned brokers that are not listed separately (Mitsubishi UFJ Morgan Stanley Securities, SMBC Nikko Securities, Mizuho Securities and others) are described with the banks instead of being ranked.

This ranking is simply an order of company size, not a recommendation or an assessment of safety.

A financial holding company founded in 2001 and headquartered in Marunouchi, Chiyoda, Tokyo. It is listed on the Tokyo, Nagoya and New York stock exchanges.[5]

Features

  • It owns a commercial bank, a trust bank and securities firms, as well as card, consumer finance and asset management companies.[5]
  • Its main subsidiaries are MUFG Bank, Mitsubishi UFJ Trust and Banking, Mitsubishi UFJ Securities Holdings and Mitsubishi UFJ NICOS; Bank of Ayudhya (Krungsri) in Thailand and Bank Danamon in Indonesia are also part of the group.[5]

Main products & services

  • Mitsubishi UFJ DirectMUFG Bank's internet banking for checking balances, making transfers and more online and in the app.
  • Foreign currency depositsDeposits held in foreign currencies such as the US dollar. They are not covered by Japan's deposit insurance, and exchange rate movements can cause losses in yen terms.

BrokerageSecurities: Mitsubishi UFJ Morgan Stanley Securities · Online broker: Mitsubishi UFJ eSmart Securities (formerly au Kabucom Securities)

04Also worth knowing

  • Fourth place and belowThe fourth-largest bank is Japan Post Bank (about ¥10.8569tn), and the fourth-largest broker is Matsui Securities (about ¥280.3bn).[1][2]
  • Exchange operator excludedJapan Exchange Group (JPX) is listed, but its core business is running the exchange rather than broking, so it is not included.
  • Bank-owned brokersMitsubishi UFJ Morgan Stanley Securities and Mitsubishi UFJ eSmart Securities (Mitsubishi UFJ), SMBC Nikko Securities (Sumitomo Mitsui) and Mizuho Securities (Mizuho) are not listed separately, so they are described with their banks rather than ranked.
  • Why SBI Holdings counts as a brokerSBI Holdings is a group that also has financial businesses outside securities, but it is the listed parent of SBI Securities, which provides broking for individuals, so it is counted as a broker.[2][14]

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Requirements to open an accountJapanese bank and securities accounts usually require an address in the country and identity checks. Whether non-residents can open one differs between companies, so check directly.
  • Deposit protectionJapan's deposit insurance protects up to ¥10 million of principal per depositor per financial institution, plus the interest on it (non-interest-bearing deposits for payments are protected in full), and does not cover foreign currency deposits. Securities accounts are covered by the investor protection fund. Korean deposit insurance does not apply.[15]
  • NISA is for residents of Japan onlyJapan's tax-exempt investment accounts (NISA) can only be opened by people living in Japan.[16]
  • Remittances and foreign exchangeWhen you send deposit or investment money abroad, the bank checks the purpose of the transaction and supporting documents. Ask your bank first whether a foreign exchange report is required.Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Overseas financial account reportingIf the combined balance of accounts held with overseas financial institutions exceeds KRW 500 million on the last day of any month, you must report it in June of the following year.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
  • Tax on deposit interestTax is often not withheld in Korea on interest from overseas deposits, so even small amounts may have to be reported for comprehensive income tax. Check with a tax professional.Korea's Income Tax Act, Article 14
  • Tax on share trading gainsGains on overseas shares bought through an overseas broker's account are treated as capital gains on overseas shares and taxed at 22% (including local income tax) after a basic annual deduction of KRW 2.5 million.
  • Limits on offering overseas fundsOverseas funds that are not registered in Korea cannot be offered or sold in Korea. This also applies to investment trusts and wrap products you see at Japanese brokers and banks.Korea's Capital Markets Act, Article 279
