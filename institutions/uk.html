01How we ranked them

The ranking uses market capitalisation (share price × shares in issue), which shows the size of a company. We used the market capitalisation published by the London Stock Exchange (LSE) at the close on 21 October 2026 as it stands.[1] Won amounts use the Bank of Korea's reference rate.[2]

Only companies headquartered in the UK are included. For brokers we counted only listed companies that offer securities accounts and dealing to individual investors. Plus500, which is listed in London but headquartered in Israel, TP ICAP, which only broker trades between institutions, the exchange operator LSEG and companies focused on asset management or advice are not included.

This ranking is simply an order of company size, not a recommendation or an assessment of safety.

A global banking group headquartered in London and also listed on HKEX. In January 2026 it made Hang Seng Bank in Hong Kong a wholly owned subsidiary.[3]

  • It has a separate 'Expat' banking service for people who live outside the UK.[5]

IG Group

IG Group Holdings plc · LSE IGG
Market capitalisation

An online trading company whose core business is spread betting and CFDs (contracts for difference). In April 2025 it bought Freetrade, a UK share investing app.[11][12]

  • CFDs and spread betting are high-risk products in which losses can grow quickly because of leverage. UK firms disclose the percentage of retail accounts that lose money.[13]

Main products & services

Broker No. 3

CMC Markets plc · LSE CMCX
Market capitalisation

An online trading company focused on CFDs and spread betting that also runs CMC Invest, for buying and holding shares directly.[13][16]

Features

  • CFDs are leveraged products: you put up only a margin deposit, but gains and losses are based on the full size of the position.[13]
  • With direct share investing (CMC Invest) you pay the full price of the shares and receive dividends and shareholder rights.[16]

Main products & services

04Also worth knowing

  • Fourth and fifth banksNatWest (£50.9bn) is fourth, and Standard Chartered (£44.2bn), which does a great deal of business in Hong Kong, is fifth.[1]
  • London-listed, headquartered elsewherePlus500 has a larger market capitalisation than AJ Bell (£2.35bn) but is headquartered in Israel, so it is not included.[1]
  • Institution-only brokersTP ICAP (£2.30bn) only brokers trades between banks and institutions and has no individual customers, so it is excluded.[1]

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Requirements to open an accountUK bank and securities accounts usually assume that you live in the UK and can verify your address. For services for people living abroad (such as HSBC Expat), check the eligibility requirements separately.
  • Deposit protectionFrom 1 December 2025 the UK's Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) protects deposits up to £120,000 per person per banking licence. Korean deposit insurance does not apply.[17]
  • ISAs are for UK residents onlyOnly UK residents can pay new money into the UK's tax-advantaged accounts (ISAs). Residents of Korea do not get this benefit.
  • Remittances and foreign exchangeWhen you send deposit or investment money abroad, the bank checks the purpose of the transaction and supporting documents. Ask your bank first whether a foreign exchange report is required.Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Overseas financial account reportingIf the combined balance of accounts held with overseas financial institutions exceeds KRW 500 million on the last day of any month, you must report it in June of the following year.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
  • Tax on share trading gainsGains on overseas shares bought through an overseas broker's account are treated as capital gains on overseas shares and taxed at 22% (including local income tax) after a basic annual deduction of KRW 2.5 million. Check separately with a tax professional how gains and losses on CFDs are taxed.
  • Limits on offering overseas fundsOverseas funds that are not registered in Korea cannot be offered or sold in Korea.Korea's Capital Markets Act, Article 279
