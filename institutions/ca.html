01How we ranked them

The ranking uses market capitalisation (share price × shares in issue), which shows the size of a company. We used the market capitalisation published by TMX, operator of the Toronto Stock Exchange, at the close on 21 October 2026.[1] Won amounts use the Bank of Korea's reference rate.[2]

Only companies headquartered in Canada are included. For brokers we counted only listed companies that offer securities accounts and dealing to individual investors. In Canada, brokers owned by the big banks, such as RBC Direct Investing, handle a large share of the securities business, and many of the online brokers that individuals use are unlisted, so bank-owned brokers are described with their banks.

This ranking is simply an order of company size, not a recommendation or an assessment of safety.

As far as we could find, Canaccord Genuity is the only listed broker headquartered in Canada, so second and third place are left empty. The brokerage services most individuals use are those of the bank-owned firms above (RBC Direct Investing, TD Direct Investing and BMO InvestorLine).

04Also worth knowing

  • Fourth-placed bankBank of Nova Scotia (C$160.5bn) is fourth, and CIBC (C$147.3bn) is fifth.[1]
  • Bank-owned brokersCanada's big banks, like the three above, own both online brokers and advice-based brokers. These are not listed separately, so they are described with their banks rather than ranked.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Requirements to open an accountCanadian bank and securities accounts usually assume that you live in the country and can verify your address and identity. Whether non-residents can open one differs between companies, so check directly.
  • Deposit protectionThe Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protects up to C$100,000 per deposit category at each member institution. Shares, funds and ETFs are not protected, and Korean deposit insurance does not apply.[9]
  • Remittances and foreign exchangeWhen you send deposit or investment money abroad, the bank checks the purpose of the transaction and supporting documents. Ask your bank first whether a foreign exchange report is required.Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Overseas financial account reportingIf the combined balance of accounts held with overseas financial institutions exceeds KRW 500 million on the last day of any month, you must report it in June of the following year.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
  • Tax on deposit interestTax is often not withheld in Korea on interest from overseas deposits, so even small amounts may have to be reported for comprehensive income tax. Check with a tax professional.Korea's Income Tax Act, Article 14
  • Tax on share trading gainsGains on overseas shares bought through an overseas broker's account are treated as capital gains on overseas shares and taxed at 22% (including local income tax) after a basic annual deduction of KRW 2.5 million.
  • Limits on offering overseas fundsOverseas funds that are not registered in Korea cannot be offered or sold in Korea.Korea's Capital Markets Act, Article 279
