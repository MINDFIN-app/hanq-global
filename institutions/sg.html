01How we ranked them

The ranking uses market capitalisation (share price × shares in issue), which shows the size of a company. We multiplied the Singapore Exchange (SGX) closing price by the latest number of shares in issue disclosed by each company; for Tiger Brokers (UP Fintech), which is listed on Nasdaq, we used the market capitalisation published by Nasdaq.[1][2][3] Won amounts use the Bank of Korea's reference rate.[4]

Only companies headquartered in Singapore are included. For brokers we counted only listed companies that offer securities accounts and dealing to individual investors. The exchange operator (SGX), unlisted brokers and bank-owned brokers that are not listed separately (DBS Vickers and OCBC Securities) are described with the banks instead of being ranked.

This ranking is simply an order of company size, not a recommendation or an assessment of safety.

02Top 3 banks by market cap

01
Bank No. 1

DBS

DBS Group Holdings · SGX D05
Market capitalisation
S$211.8bn
approx. KRW 220.1tn

Singapore's largest banking group by market capitalisation. It serves personal customers under two brands, DBS and POSB.[5]

Features

  • Its flagship product is a 'relationship-linked' account that pays more interest when you bring your salary, cards, investments, insurance and loans together.[5]
  • Online share dealing is handled by its broking arm, DBS Vickers.[6]

Main products & services

  • DBS Multiplier AccountA current account that pays higher interest the more you use cards, invest, take out insurance and borrow for a home, on top of crediting your salary.
  • digiPortfolioA hybrid robo-advisory investment product in which algorithms manage portfolios built by experts.

Singapore's second-largest banking group. Its flagship products are the 360 Account, which pays bonus interest based on your banking activity, and a robo-investing service for small amounts.[7][8]

  • Online share dealing is handled by its broking arm, OCBC Securities (iOCBC).[9]

  • OCBC 360 AccountAn account that pays bonus interest for crediting your salary, growing your balance, spending on cards and taking out insurance or investments.

One of Singapore's three big banks. Its flagship services are the One Account, which pays more interest when you meet both a card spending and a salary credit condition, and the UOB TMRW mobile app.[10][11]

04Also worth knowing

  • Exchange operator excludedSingapore Exchange (SGX), the exchange operator, is listed, but its core business is running the exchange rather than broking, so it is not included.
  • Listing venue and head officeTiger Brokers (UP Fintech) is listed on Nasdaq but headquartered in Singapore, so it is on this page. moomoo, which operates in Singapore, belongs to Futu, which is headquartered in Hong Kong, so it is on the Hong Kong page.
  • Bank-owned brokersDBS Vickers and OCBC Securities are not listed separately, so they are described with their banks rather than ranked.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Requirements to open an accountSingapore bank and securities accounts usually require you to apply in person and verify your address and identity. Whether non-residents can open one differs between companies, so check directly.
  • Deposit protectionSingapore's deposit insurance (SDIC) protects up to S$100,000 per depositor per bank, and only Singapore dollar deposits. Foreign currency deposits and investment products are not protected, and Korean deposit insurance does not apply.[18]
  • Remittances and foreign exchangeWhen you send deposit or investment money abroad, the bank checks the purpose of the transaction and supporting documents. Ask your bank first whether a foreign exchange report is required.Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Overseas financial account reportingIf the combined balance of accounts held with overseas financial institutions exceeds KRW 500 million on the last day of any month, you must report it in June of the following year.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
  • Tax on deposit interestTax is often not withheld in Korea on interest from overseas deposits, so even small amounts may have to be reported for comprehensive income tax. Check with a tax professional.Korea's Income Tax Act, Article 14
  • Tax on share trading gainsGains on overseas shares bought through an overseas broker's account are treated as capital gains on overseas shares and taxed at 22% (including local income tax) after a basic annual deduction of KRW 2.5 million.
  • Limits on offering overseas fundsOverseas funds that are not registered in Korea cannot be offered or sold in Korea. This also applies to funds and robo-advisory products you see on local platforms.Korea's Capital Markets Act, Article 279
