Hong Kong · Representative participating savings plan

WealthAhead II Savings Insurance – Supreme

AXA China Region Insurance Company (Hong Kong) Limited

AXA Hong Kong's participating savings plan. Non-guaranteed bonuses build up on top of a guaranteed surrender value, and with up to 9 currencies and tools for splitting and passing on the policy, it is designed for transferring assets over the long term.

Premium term
5 or 10 years
Premium rate fixed for the whole term
Policy currencies
Up to 9
USD · HKD · RMB · GBP · EUR · CAD · AUD · SGD · MOP
Minimum notional amount
USD 10,000
HKD 80,000 · RMB 65,000

01Product overview

This is the Supreme version, the first one presented in the WealthAhead II Savings Insurance series (Supreme and Prime) at the top of the savings plans list on AXA Hong Kong's website. The brochure describes it as a long-term savings plan that pools premiums in a participating fund and shares the results as bonuses.[1][2]

Cover runs until the insured person reaches 138. The entry age is 0–60 (Superior) or 0–70 (Regular), depending on the death benefit option. A guaranteed surrender value builds up from the 3rd year, and non-guaranteed bonuses may be added once the policy has been in force for 3 years.[2]

02How it works

STEP 1
Paying premiums
You pay for 5 or 10 years. The premium rate does not change during the premium term.
STEP 2
The participating fund
Premiums are pooled in the company's participating fund and invested in bonds, equities and other assets.
STEP 3
Bonus declarations
Each year a reversionary bonus (its face value is guaranteed once declared) and a terminal bonus settled on surrender or death are set. Both are non-guaranteed.
STEP 4
Locking in, withdrawing, passing on
From the 5th year you can move part of the value into an account to lock it in, and pass the policy to family through regular withdrawals, splitting the policy or changing the insured person.

03Key features

Up to 9 currencies

You choose the policy currency from RMB, USD, GBP, EUR, CAD, AUD, SGD, HKD and MOP (MOP for policies issued in Macau only), with an option to change to another currency later.

Policy Value Lock-in

From the 5th policy anniversary, each year you can move 10–50% of the guaranteed surrender value and the cash value of bonuses into a Prime Currency Account to lock it in. Interest on the account is non-guaranteed.

Dual-currency accounts

Part of the locked-in amount can be moved into a Global Currency Account in a currency other than the policy currency, and you can move money between the two accounts.

Wealth Master Service

You can arrange in advance for regular withdrawals to be paid out to up to 3 people.

Policy splitting and succession tools

The plan offers policy splitting (Flexi Segregation), changing the insured person, a successor insured (Flexi Continuation), a successor policyholder (Heritage Protector), and Flexi Care, which lets a named person make withdrawals if the policyholder loses mental capacity.

Death benefit

Pays the higher of 100% of premiums paid (plus an extra 30% on the Superior option if the insured person is 60 or under and the policy has been in force for 3 years) and the total of the guaranteed surrender value, the face value of bonuses and the account values.

04Risks and points to note

  • Bonuses are not promisesReversionary and terminal bonuses and account interest are non-guaranteed. Actual amounts may be higher or lower than in the illustration you received when you applied.
  • Losses on early surrenderThe plan is designed to be held for the long term, so if you surrender early you may get back less than you paid.
  • Currency switching and exchange ratesWhen you change currency or move money into an account in another currency, the exchange rate set by the company is used. The value in won depends on exchange rates.
  • Insurer credit and governing lawThe ability to pay depends on the financial strength of AXA's Hong Kong company, and the contract is governed by Hong Kong law.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Limits on offering and arranging it in KoreaBeing offered this product in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary, is restricted in principle. The exceptions are narrowly defined in the Enforcement Decree.Korea's Insurance Business Act, Article 3
  • Applying in Hong KongHong Kong insurers usually take applications and signatures in Hong Kong itself. Each insurer has its own rules on accepting non-residents.
  • Remittance and foreign exchangeWhen you send premiums abroad, your bank will check the purpose of the transaction and supporting documents. Ask your bank first whether a foreign exchange report is required.
  • Tax and reportingCheck with a tax adviser how surrender payouts and bonuses are taxed in Korea and whether the policy must be reported as an overseas financial account.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
