Money market funds

Money market funds · IMAS / Morningstar

Funds that invest in short-term deposits and short-dated bonds and are used much like cash. Nearly 60% of Singapore's fund net inflows in the first half of 2025 went into them.

01Why it sells

According to the quarterly reports of the Investment Management Association of Singapore (IMAS) and Morningstar, nearly 60% of the S$6.3bn of fund net inflows in the first half of 2025 went into money market funds. S$2.8bn came in during Q2 alone, about three times Q1, falling to S$1.5bn in Q3.[2][3]

The reports do not give a separate full-year total, and from Q4 the money market category was replaced by 'Capital Preservation', which recorded −S$690m. Net inflows in Q2 and Q3 alone came to S$4.3bn, more than equity funds' full-year net inflows (S$2.17bn), so we placed it second. Demand for parking spare cash at high short-term interest rates drove the first-half flows.[1]

02How it works

STEP 1
Buying the fund
You buy a fund in the currency you want, such as Singapore or US dollars.
STEP 2
Short-term assets
The fund spreads its money across bank deposits and short-dated government bonds and bills.
STEP 3
Interest
Short-term interest builds up little by little in the fund's price every day.
STEP 4
Redeeming any time
When you need the money, you redeem and quickly turn it back into cash.

03Key features

High liquidity

You can redeem at short notice, which makes these funds a good place to park spare cash.

Low volatility

Holding only short-dated assets keeps price swings small.

Choice of currency

Besides Singapore dollar funds, you can choose US dollar money market funds.

04Risks and points to note

  • No guarantee of capitalThese are funds, not deposits, so they are not covered by deposit insurance, and losses can occur, though rarely.
  • Falling interest ratesWhen short-term rates fall, returns fall straight away.
  • Credit of the assets heldThe fund is affected by the creditworthiness of the institutions that issue the deposits and bills it holds.
  • Exchange ratesWith foreign-currency money market funds, exchange rate moves can matter more than the return, measured in won.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Registration in KoreaOverseas funds that are not registered in Korea cannot be offered or sold in Korea.Korea's Capital Markets Act, Article 279
  • TaxDistributions and gains on redemption from overseas funds are generally taxed as dividend income, and if your annual financial income exceeds KRW 20 million it is taxed under comprehensive income taxation.
  • Overseas financial account reportingIf the combined balance of accounts held with overseas financial institutions exceeds KRW 500 million on the last day of any month, you must report it in June of the following year.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
  • Compare Korean alternativesFor short-term money, compare returns, conversion costs and convenience with foreign-currency deposits and foreign-currency money market products in Korea.
