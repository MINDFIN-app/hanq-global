02

Global equity investment trusts including Japan

内外株式型 · JITA category

Publicly offered investment trusts that hold Japanese and overseas shares in a single fund. Funds that spread their money across shares around the world, Japan included, fall into this category. Net inflows of ¥4.6490tn in 2025 put it second, up 35.5% over the year.

01Why it sells

In the statistics of the Investment Trusts Association, Japan, Japanese-and-overseas equity funds drew net inflows of ¥4.6490tn in 2025, second only to overseas equity funds. That is 32.5% of the ¥14.3188tn total for publicly offered equity investment trusts and 35.5% more than in 2024 (¥3.4305tn), the largest rise in net inflows among the top three.[1]

Subscriptions in the same year were ¥10.8030tn and redemptions ¥6.1378tn, and net assets reached ¥40.7497tn at the year end, up 31.7% over the year. In the same year, net inflows into overseas equity funds fell by ¥2.5162tn, while those into Japanese-and-overseas equity funds rose by ¥1.2185tn.[1]

02How it works

STEP 1
Buying the fund
You buy in yen through a Japanese broker or bank, often through a regular monthly savings plan.
STEP 2
Spreading across the world
The manager invests in shares in Japan and many other countries. Index funds follow the country and company weightings of their index.
STEP 3
Reflected in the unit price
Movements in share prices around the world and in exchange rates are reflected in the yen unit price every day.
STEP 4
Redemption
When you ask to redeem, you receive yen a few days later.

03Key features

A global spread in one fund

You invest in stock markets around the world at once, without having to choose several single-country funds.

Includes Japanese shares

Unlike overseas equity funds, they also hold Japanese shares, so part of the money is in yen assets.

Weightings adjust themselves

In index funds, weightings change on their own as each country's market capitalisation changes, so there is less need to rebalance yourself.

04Risks and points to note

  • No capital guaranteeThey invest in shares, so you can lose money if stock markets around the world fall together.
  • Exchange ratesThe overseas share portion is affected by exchange rates in yen terms, and in won terms the won/yen rate is added on top.
  • Index concentrationMarket-cap-weighted indices give large markets such as the US a high weighting, so even a 'global' fund is strongly affected by particular markets.
  • CostsHolding costs such as trust fees are deducted from the unit price every day. Costs differ between funds, even for the same index.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Registration in KoreaOverseas funds that are not registered in Korea cannot be offered or sold in Korea.Korea's Capital Markets Act, Article 279
  • NISA is for residents of Japan onlyJapan's tax-exempt investment accounts (NISA) can only be opened by people living in Japan. Residents of Korea do not get this benefit.Financial Services Agency of Japan · NISA guide
  • TaxDistributions and gains on redemption from overseas funds are generally taxed as dividend income, and if your annual financial income exceeds KRW 20 million it is taxed under comprehensive income taxation.
  • Overseas financial account reportingIf the combined balance of accounts held with overseas financial institutions exceeds KRW 500 million on the last day of any month, you must report it in June of the following year.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
