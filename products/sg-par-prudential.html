Singapore · Representative participating whole life plan

PRUWealth Income II (USD)

Prudential Assurance Company Singapore (Pte) Limited

Prudential Singapore's US-dollar participating whole life plan. You pay premiums for 3, 5 or 8 years and receive monthly income for life from the 4th year, and it is designed to pass to the next generation through splitting the policy (Wealth Share) and changing the insured person.

Policy currency
USD
US-dollar policy
Premium term
3 · 5 · 8 years
Regular premiums
Monthly income starts
From year 4
Increases from year 6, or a larger increase deferred to year 11

01Product overview

In MAS's 2025 insurance company returns (Form L1), Prudential Singapore ranked third for weighted participating new business premiums, at S$548m. If single premiums are counted in full, it is the largest of the three.[1] The first product in the savings list on the company's official website (PRUAssure Income (USD)) is a single-premium plan with guaranteed benefits whose brochure does not describe it as participating, so we show this participating plan, which is second.[2]

The company describes it as a regular-premium participating whole life plan. Monthly income is made up of a guaranteed part and a non-guaranteed part, each calculated as a percentage of a notional face value, and a non-guaranteed performance bonus is added separately.[3]

02How it works

STEP 1
Paying premiums
You pay in US dollars for the term you choose: 3, 5 or 8 years.
STEP 2
The participating fund
Premiums are pooled in Prudential's participating fund and invested, and its performance feeds into the non-guaranteed income and the performance bonus.
STEP 3
Monthly income
From the 4th year you receive monthly income for life. It increases from the 6th year, or by more if you defer the increase to the 11th year. You can also leave it with the company to earn non-guaranteed interest.
STEP 4
Passing on and splitting
After the premium term you can pass the policy to the next generation by splitting it (Wealth Share) or changing the insured person. The policy can continue until the original insured person would reach 380.

03Key features

Monthly income

Made up of a guaranteed and a non-guaranteed cash benefit, both calculated as a percentage of the face value. The face value is a notional amount used only to calculate payouts.

Choosing when income rises

You choose between monthly income rising from the 6th year, or deferring the increase to the 11th year for a bigger rise.

Wealth Share and changing the insured

After the premium term you can split the policy or change the insured person (insurable interest and the company's approval are needed). The policy and any sub-policies end when the original insured person would reach 380.

Secondary owner, secondary insured, joint owner

You can name a secondary policyowner from 2 years after the start, and add a secondary insured or a joint owner so the policy continues if something happens.

Death benefit in instalments

The death benefit is the higher of 101% of premiums paid and 101% of the surrender value (on accidental death, 105% of premiums paid is used for the comparison), and you can choose to have it paid in instalments over up to 10 years.

Disability waiver and retrenchment benefit

During the premium term (the first 5 years for the 3-year term) and before age 65, premiums are waived on disability and a retrenchment benefit (up to S$100,000 per policyowner) applies.

Optional riders

Crisis Waiver (USD) waives premiums on diagnosis of one of 35 critical illnesses, and Payer Security (USD) waives premiums on a child's policy if the payer dies, becomes disabled or has a critical illness (5 and 8-year terms only).

Policy owners' protection

The policy is protected up to the set limits under the policy owners' protection scheme run by the Singapore Deposit Insurance Corporation (SDIC).

04Risks and points to note

  • The non-guaranteed part of incomeThe non-guaranteed part of monthly income, the performance bonus and interest on income left with the company are set by the company and may be lower than in the brochure's examples.
  • Losses on early surrenderIt is a long-term contract, so surrendering early is costly and the surrender value may be less than the premiums you paid, or nothing.
  • A US-dollar policyPremiums and income are both in US dollars, so the value in won depends on exchange rates.
  • Entry conditions not publishedEntry ages and the minimum premium are not on the official website or in the brochure (no data found). Check the product summary before applying.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Limits on offering and arranging it in KoreaBeing offered this product in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary, is restricted in principle.Korea's Insurance Business Act, Article 3
  • Local application requirementsRules on selling to non-residents vary by insurer, and many require you to apply in person in Singapore.
  • Remittance and foreign exchangeYou will be sending premiums abroad for many years, so check remittance costs and the bank's procedures for confirming the purpose of transactions in advance.
  • Tax and reportingCheck with a tax adviser how maturity and surrender payouts and bonuses are taxed in Korea, and whether the policy must be reported as an overseas financial account.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
  • Tax on insurance gainsIf what you receive on surrender or maturity exceeds the premiums you paid, the gain is taxed in Korea as interest income, and the special rule that excludes Korean savings policies held for 10 years or more from tax does not apply to overseas policies taken out under the proviso to Article 3 of the Insurance Business Act.Korea's Income Tax Act, Article 16 · Enforcement Decree, Article 25
