Hong Kong · Representative participating savings plan

Chubb MyLegacy Multi-Currency Insurance Plan

Chubb Life · Hong Kong

Chubb Life Hong Kong's participating whole life plan. You pay for just 2 or 5 years, can change currency from the 3rd year or lock in part of the terminal bonus from the 10th year, and can name successor policyholders and insured persons widely.

Premium term
2 or 5 years
Monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annual
Policy currencies
USD · HKD
Can switch to another currency from the 3rd year
Minimum notional amount
USD 10,000
For the 2-year term · HKD 78,000

01Product overview

This is the first product in the 'Wealth Planning' list on Chubb Hong Kong's website (followed by MyLegacy V, Future Achiever II and Forever Diamond II). The brochure presents it as a participating whole life insurance plan.[1][2]

Cover runs until the insured person reaches 120, and the entry age is 15 days to 75. Value builds up in three strands: the guaranteed surrender value, a non-guaranteed reversionary bonus and a non-guaranteed terminal bonus. The company states a target asset mix for this plan of 20–100% in bonds and similar assets and 0–80% in equities and similar assets.[2]

02How it works

STEP 1
Paying premiums
You pay for 2 or 5 years. The premium rate is fixed for the whole premium term.
STEP 2
The participating fund
Money from participating products is pooled and invested in US and Asian assets, targeting 20–100% in bonds and similar assets and 0–80% in equities and similar assets.
STEP 3
Bonus declarations
The reversionary bonus is set each year at the company's discretion, and its face value is guaranteed once declared. The terminal bonus is non-guaranteed.
STEP 4
Switching, withdrawing, passing on
You adapt your plans through currency switching, locking in the terminal bonus, regular withdrawals, splitting the policy and changing the insured person.

03Key features

Currency switching

From the 3rd policy anniversary, once a year (within the 30 days before the anniversary) you can switch to another currency. There is no fee and no limit on how many times you apply.

Locking in the terminal bonus

From the 10th policy anniversary you can move 10–50% of the cash value of the terminal bonus into a settlement deposit account to earn non-guaranteed interest or withdraw it.

Changing the insured person

From the 1st policy anniversary you can change the insured person any number of times.

Naming successors

You can name up to 5 successor insured persons and up to 5 successor policyholders, in order. If a minor child is a successor policyholder, you can appoint up to 5 interim policyholders (Policy Guardian).

Policy splitting and regular withdrawals

You can turn part of the surrender value into a separate policy and set up regular withdrawal instructions in advance.

Easy to apply and pay

If you meet the brochure's conditions, there is no medical underwriting. On the 5-year term you can apply for a premium holiday of up to 2 years after paying for 2 years.

04Risks and points to note

  • Bonuses are not promisesNon-guaranteed bonuses are set by the company based on its experience of investment returns, claims, surrenders and expenses, and actual amounts may be higher or lower than illustrated.
  • Losses on early surrenderThe brochure states that if you surrender early, the surrender value may be less than the premiums paid and you may make a significant loss.
  • Risk if premiums stopIf a premium is not paid on time, cover can end and the policy can be terminated after a 31-day grace period.
  • Insurer credit and exchange ratesThe policy is exposed to Chubb Life's credit risk. There is exchange rate risk when you switch currency or convert into won.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Limits on offering and arranging it in KoreaBeing offered this product in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary, is restricted in principle. The exceptions are narrowly defined in the Enforcement Decree.Korea's Insurance Business Act, Article 3
  • Applying in Hong KongHong Kong insurers usually take applications and signatures in Hong Kong itself. Each insurer has its own rules on accepting non-residents.
  • Remittance and foreign exchangeWhen you send premiums abroad, your bank will check the purpose of the transaction and supporting documents. Ask your bank first whether a foreign exchange report is required.
  • Tax and reportingCheck with a tax adviser how surrender payouts and bonuses are taxed in Korea and whether the policy must be reported as an overseas financial account.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
