01Why it sells

In the Life Insurance Association Singapore's (LIA) full-year results for 2025, non-participating products made up 32% of weighted new business premiums. The amount was not published, but taking investment-linked business of S$2.88bn and participating business of S$1.56bn away from the S$6.53bn total gives approx. S$2.09bn. It ranks second by share, but fell 4.8% in a year.[1]

Demand for protection itself was strong. In the same year, weighted new business for individual health insurance was S$743m (+19.0%), of which Integrated Shield Plans (IPs) and riders accounted for S$666m (+19.6%), or 90% of individual health insurance. Some 3 million people hold an IP, about 71% of Singapore residents.[1]

02How it works

STEP 1
Choosing the cover
You choose what is covered (death, critical illness, hospital stays and so on), the amount and the period.
STEP 2
Disclosure and underwriting
You declare your health and are underwritten. Depending on the result, the premium or scope of cover may change.
STEP 3
Paying premiums
You pay the agreed premium. With health insurance, the premium is often reset at renewal.
STEP 4
Claims
If an accident, diagnosis or hospital stay set out in the policy terms occurs, the agreed benefit is paid.

03Key features

Fixed cover

Benefits are set regardless of investment performance, which makes planning easy.

Topping up public health cover

Integrated Shield Plans (IPs) sit on top of the national health insurance scheme (MediShield Life) and widen your choice of ward and hospital.

Low premiums

With little savings element, the same premium buys more cover.

04Risks and points to note

  • Higher premiums on renewalHealth insurance premiums can rise at renewal with age and rising medical costs.
  • Little or no surrender valueProtection-focused products return almost nothing if you cancel.
  • Different policy definitionsDefinitions of critical illness, waiting periods and pre-existing condition exclusions vary by product.
  • Residency requirementsIntegrated Shield Plans are linked to MediShield Life, so they are for Singapore citizens and permanent residents. Foreigners need to look for ordinary health insurance.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Limits on offering and arranging it in KoreaBeing offered this product in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary, is restricted in principle.Korea's Insurance Business Act, Article 3
  • Check who can applyMany Singapore health insurance products assume you live in Singapore. First check whether non-residents can apply.
  • Overlap with Korean insuranceCompare whether the cover overlaps with Korean indemnity or diagnosis-benefit insurance. Indemnity-type cover may not pay twice for the same costs.
  • Remittance and reportingWhen sending premiums abroad, check the bank's questions about the purpose of the transaction and whether you must report the policy as an overseas financial account.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
