United Kingdom · Representative participating product

PruFund (Prudential Investment Plan)

The Prudential Assurance Company Limited · M&G plc

PruFund is the with-profits fund range of Prudential in the UK (now part of the M&G group). Money is pooled in Prudential's With-Profits Fund and spread across shares, property, bonds and other assets, and a 'smoothing' process that raises the unit price every day by an expected growth rate set by the company reduces short-term market ups and downs. UK residents invest through an investment bond, an ISA or a pension.

Minimum investment
£10,000
Single payment into the Prudential Investment Plan · top-ups also from £10,000
Suggested investment period
5 to 10 years or more
As stated by the company
Expected growth rate review
Every 3 months
25 Feb, May, Aug and Nov · can go up or down

01Product overview

In the UK, regulators do not publish participating (with-profits) new business by company. In a 2019 report the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) said with-profits business is mainly legacy business in run-off, closed to new customers.[1] According to two trade publications reporting 2023 data from the analyst AKG, Prudential accounted for 83% (£6.3bn) of the £7.6bn of UK with-profits net premiums that year.[2] So for the UK we show this one company's product rather than a top three.

PruFund is the first with-profits fund in the funds and products menu of the company's official website, and is available through pensions, ISAs and investment bonds. Here we describe it on the terms of the Prudential Investment Plan, an investment bond and the first insurance product in the savings and investments list that offers PruFund (the OEIC funds at the top of the list are not insurance and do not include PruFund).[3][4] The company says that at the end of 2025 there was £70bn in PruFund from 500,000 investors, and that the Prudential With-Profits Fund it belongs to holds £134bn.[3]

The provider, The Prudential Assurance Company Limited, is a subsidiary of the UK's M&G plc and is not affiliated with Prudential plc, the group Prudential Singapore belongs to, or with Prudential Financial in the US. The former official website pru.co.uk now leads to mandg.com, and product documents use the names Pru, Prudential and M&G together.[3][7]

02How it works

STEP 1
Apply through an adviser
PruFund is only available through a financial adviser. A UK resident aged over 18 puts at least £10,000 as a single payment into the investment bond (Prudential Investment Plan).
STEP 2
Holding account → investment date
Your money grows at the expected growth rate in a holding account until the next investment date (25 February, May, August or November), when it buys units in the PruFund fund you chose.
STEP 3
Smoothing
The unit price rises every day by the daily equivalent of the expected growth rate. If the gap with the 'unsmoothed price', based on the actual value of the assets, goes beyond set limits, the company adjusts the price.
STEP 4
Withdrawals and cashing in
You can take partial or regular withdrawals or cash in, but money taken out of PruFund may be paid up to 28 days after your request, at the price on the 28th day.

03Key features

Fund range

PruFund Cautious holds more bonds, PruFund Growth spreads across shares, property, bonds and more, Risk Managed 1 to 5 manage volatility within set limits, and Planet 1 to 5 add environmental and social criteria to the Risk Managed range.

Expected growth rate (EGR)

The company's directors set it for each fund every 3 months, looking at expected returns over up to 15 years, and it can go up or down. We have not included the rates because they change every quarter.

Smoothing limits

Each day, if the gap with the unsmoothed price (and its 5-working-day average) reaches the daily limit (Growth 10%, Cautious 8%), the gap is cut to 2.5% or 2.0%; on investment dates, if it reaches the limit (Growth 5%, Cautious 4%), the gap is halved (guide of June 2026).

Price resets and suspended smoothing

In highly unusual circumstances the company may, to protect the With-Profits Fund, set the unit price to the unsmoothed price (a Unit Price Reset) or suspend smoothing for some days so the price follows the actual value.

Death benefit and guarantee options

The investment bond is a life insurance policy, so if the life assured dies it pays 100.1% of the fund value. Where available when you start, you can add a PruFund Protected fund, which guarantees a minimum amount at the end of a chosen term, or an option so the death benefit is not less than what you paid in (less withdrawals and adviser charges); each has its own charge.

Charges

The Annual Management Charge (AMC) depends on the funds you choose and the amount you first invest, and for PruFund it is taken by cancelling units each month. The rate for each fund is in the key information documents and personal illustration, and we could not find it on public pages (no data found). Adviser charges are agreed separately with your adviser.

Shareholders' share

Up to one ninth of any increase in the value of a plan's holding in PruFund goes to the company's shareholders, and the company says this is already paid for out of the charges.

UK compensation scheme

If Prudential Assurance were in default, the PruFund and With-Profits holdings in your plan are protected 100% by the UK Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS). Losses from investment performance are not covered.

04Risks and points to note

  • Losing moneySmoothing does not stop losses. The value can fall, and if returns are lower than the charges you get back less than you paid in.
  • A sudden price cutIf markets fall sharply, the unit price can be adjusted down in one go. The unsmoothed price is not published, so this is hard to see coming.
  • Delays on withdrawals and switchesTaking money out of PruFund or switching to another fund can take up to 28 days (occasionally a day more), and the price can change in the meantime.
  • The company's discretionThe company sets and can change the expected growth rates, smoothing limits, charges and whether smoothing is suspended.
  • A sterling planYou pay in and are paid in pounds sterling, so the value in won depends on exchange rates.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Limits on offering and arranging it in KoreaBeing offered this product in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary, is restricted in principle.Korea's Insurance Business Act, Article 3
  • Who can applyOnly UK residents aged over 18 can start a Prudential Investment Plan, and the company says it cannot accept new money from customers who are not physically resident in the UK. If you leave the UK you can no longer top up. Korea is also not on the list of countries served by Prudential International (Ireland), the arm for people living outside the UK (checked 2026-10-26).
  • Remittance and foreign exchangeWhen you send money abroad to invest, your bank will check the purpose of the transaction and supporting documents. Ask your bank first whether a foreign exchange report is required.
  • Tax and reportingCheck with a tax adviser how maturity and surrender payouts and bonuses are taxed in Korea, and whether the policy must be reported as an overseas financial account.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
  • Tax on insurance gainsIf what you receive on surrender or maturity exceeds the premiums you paid, the gain is taxed in Korea as interest income, and the special rule that excludes Korean savings policies held for 10 years or more from tax does not apply to overseas policies taken out under the proviso to Article 3 of the Insurance Business Act.Korea's Income Tax Act, Article 16 · Enforcement Decree, Article 25
