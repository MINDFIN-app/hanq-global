Participating policies

Participating whole life & endowment

Savings and whole life insurance that shares the investment performance of the insurer's participating fund through bonuses. It is used for long-term goals such as retirement and education, and made up 24% of new business in Singapore in 2025.

01Why it sells

In the Life Insurance Association Singapore's (LIA) full-year results for 2025, weighted new business premiums for participating products rose 9.6%, from S$1.43bn to S$1.56bn. That is 24% of all new business, third after investment-linked and non-participating products.[1]

The LIA said Singaporeans were looking after both protection and wealth building despite market volatility. Participating products appeal to people who want long-term savings that swing less than investment-linked policies, which take market performance in full. In the same year, insurers paid out S$12.17bn on maturing policies.[1]

02How it works

STEP 1
Paying premiums
You pay regular or single premiums, which are pooled in the insurer's participating fund (par fund).
STEP 2
The participating fund
The insurer invests in bonds, equities and other assets, and policyholders share in the results.
STEP 3
Bonus declarations
Bonuses that build up each year and bonuses settled at maturity or surrender are set. They are not guaranteed.
STEP 4
Maturity, surrender or cover
At maturity or surrender you receive the guaranteed amount plus bonuses; on death, the death benefit is paid.

03Key features

Two return illustrations

Singapore participating products show results under two investment return assumptions, one higher and one lower, in the illustration given when you apply.

Smoothing

The insurer holds back part of the gains in good years to make up for bad years, reducing swings in bonuses.

Long-term goals

They are designed around long-term goals such as retirement income, children's education and inheritance.

04Risks and points to note

  • Bonuses are not promisesThe illustrated returns are only assumptions. Actual bonuses can be lower depending on investment performance.
  • Losses on early surrenderIf you surrender early, you usually get back less than you paid.
  • Exchange ratesPolicies are in Singapore or US dollars, so the value in won depends on exchange rates.
  • Insurer creditThe ability to pay depends on the insurer's financial strength. Check information on the insurer's financial soundness.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Limits on offering and arranging it in KoreaBeing offered this product in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary, is restricted in principle.Korea's Insurance Business Act, Article 3
  • Local application requirementsRules on selling to non-residents vary by insurer, and many require you to apply in person in Singapore.
  • Remittance and foreign exchangeYou will be sending premiums abroad for many years, so check remittance costs and the bank's procedures for confirming the purpose of transactions in advance.
  • Tax and reportingCheck with a tax adviser how maturity and surrender payouts and bonuses are taxed in Korea, and whether the policy must be reported as an overseas financial account.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
