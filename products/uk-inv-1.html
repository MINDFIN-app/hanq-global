UK · Investment No. 1
01

Money market funds

Money market · Investment Association asset class

Funds that invest in very short-dated government bonds, deposits and commercial paper. In 2025 money market was the asset class with the highest net retail sales in the UK, and recorded its largest annual inflow on record.

01Why it sells

According to the Investment Association (IA), the UK retail fund market as a whole saw net outflows of £2.3bn in 2025, but £6.9bn went into money market funds, a record for the asset class. By sector, Short Term Money Market was the best seller at £6.1bn.[1]

The IA's analysis was that investors moved out of US and global equity strategies into diversified, lower-risk assets such as money market, mixed-asset and mixed bond funds. Equity funds saw outflows of £16.8bn in the same year.[1]

02How it works

STEP 1
Buying the fund
You buy a pound (or dollar or euro) money market fund through an investment platform or bank.
STEP 2
Short-term assets
The fund spreads its money across short-dated government bonds, deposits and high-quality commercial paper.
STEP 3
Interest
Interest at around short-term rates builds up every day.
STEP 4
Redeeming any time
When you need the money, you redeem and turn it into cash quickly.

03Key features

High liquidity

You can redeem at short notice, so these funds are used to hold spare cash.

Low volatility

Holding only short-dated, high-quality assets keeps price swings small.

Held in tax-efficient accounts

UK residents often hold them in an Individual Savings Account (ISA) so the interest is not taxed.

04Risks and points to note

  • No guarantee of capitalThese are funds, not deposits, so they are not covered by deposit protection.
  • Falling interest ratesWhen the Bank of England lowers its base rate, returns fall straight away.
  • InflationIf returns are below the rate of inflation, the real value falls.
  • Exchange ratesThey are in pounds, so measured in won, exchange rate moves can matter more than the interest.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Registration in KoreaOverseas funds that are not registered in Korea cannot be offered or sold in Korea.Korea's Capital Markets Act, Article 279
  • ISAs are for UK residentsOnly UK residents can pay new money into the UK's tax-free ISA. Korean residents do not get this benefit.
  • TaxDistributions and gains on redemption from overseas funds are generally taxed as dividend income, and if your annual financial income exceeds KRW 20 million it is taxed under comprehensive income taxation.
  • Overseas financial account reportingIf the combined balance of accounts held with overseas financial institutions exceeds KRW 500 million on the last day of any month, you must report it in June of the following year.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
