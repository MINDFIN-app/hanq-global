Nippon Life (日本生命)

ニッセイ みらいのカタチ 終身保険

Whole life insurance
  • Ages 3–75 at entry, lifelong death cover
  • Builds wealth through a surrender value
  • After premiums are paid up, the death cover can be converted into an annuity
Official product page →

01Why it sells

According to the combined figures for the 41 members of the Life Insurance Association of Japan, new whole life policies in FY2025 numbered 2.269m (18.1%), second only to medical insurance. The count fell 1.8%, but new death cover written rose 12.1% to ¥14.5736tn, 24.7% of all new cover.[1]

Among policies in force, whole life accounts for 38.66m policies and ¥224.4813tn of death cover, 28.8% of all individual cover in force.[1] The association says that in FY2024 new policies by age, people in their 50s and those aged 60 and over chose whole life more than any type except 'other', such as medical insurance.[2]

02How it works

STEP 1
Paying premiums
You pay premiums up to a set age or for life. Besides yen, some products are set in foreign currencies such as the US dollar.
STEP 2
Building surrender value
Part of each premium is set aside as policy reserves, so the surrender value grows over time.
STEP 3
Lifelong cover
Cover continues for life even after all premiums have been paid.
STEP 4
Payout
When the insured person dies, the beneficiary receives the set death benefit.

03Key features

Lifelong cover

Unlike term insurance, whose cover ends with the term, there is no end date, so it is used to prepare for inheritance and funeral costs.

Surrender value

The surrender value grows as premiums build up, so it also works as savings.

Currency and surrender designs

Plans include yen and foreign-currency versions, and 'low surrender value' types that cut premiums by keeping the surrender value low during the payment period.

04Risks and points to note

  • Loss on early surrenderIf you surrender early, the surrender value is often less than the premiums paid, so you can lose money.
  • Fixed assumed interest ratePremiums and surrender values are calculated at the assumed rate set when you join, and the terms stay the same even if interest rates later rise.
  • Exchange ratesForeign-currency plans carry exchange rate risk even in yen terms, and in won terms they are exposed to two exchange rate movements.
  • Insurer's creditIf the insurer fails, the Life Insurance Policyholders Protection Corporation of Japan protects policies, but benefits and surrender values may be reduced.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Limits on offering and arranging it in KoreaBeing offered this product in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary, is restricted in principle.Korea's Insurance Business Act, Article 3
  • For residents of JapanJapanese life insurance is generally sold to people living in Japan. It is best read as information for family members living in Japan.
  • InheritanceCheck with a tax adviser how overseas insurance payouts are included in an estate in Korea and how they are reported.
  • Overseas financial account reportingCheck whether accounts and policies held with overseas financial institutions must be reported.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
  • Tax on insurance gainsIf what you receive on surrender or maturity exceeds the premiums you paid, the gain is taxed in Korea as interest income, and the special rule that excludes Korean savings policies held for 10 years or more from tax does not apply to overseas policies taken out under the proviso to Article 3 of the Insurance Business Act.Korea's Income Tax Act, Article 16 · Enforcement Decree, Article 25
