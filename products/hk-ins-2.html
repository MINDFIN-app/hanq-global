01Why it sells

In the Insurance Authority's provisional statistics for 2025, individual non-linked new business premiums were HK$312.1bn (+49.9%). Taking away participating business of HK$282.8bn leaves HK$29.3bn in non-participating products, 8.9% of long-term new business and second place.[1][2]

The amount is far smaller than for participating plans, but this category is mainly about buying protection rather than putting in large sums to grow, so the premium per policy is small. Among Hong Kong's protection products, critical illness (CI) insurance, which pays a lump sum on diagnosis, is the best known, and some plans are designed to allow several claims.

02How it works

STEP 1
Choosing the cover
You choose what is covered (death, critical illness, hospital stays and so on), the sum insured and the period of cover.
STEP 2
Disclosure and underwriting
You declare your health and have a medical check if needed. Depending on the result, the premium may change or some cover may be excluded.
STEP 3
Paying premiums
You pay the agreed premium. On renewable plans, the premium is reset for your age at each renewal.
STEP 4
Claims
If an event or diagnosis set out in the policy terms occurs, the agreed benefit is paid. There are no bonuses.

03Key features

Cover fixed from the start

Benefits and surrender values are set in the contract regardless of investment performance, so the product is easy to understand.

Low premiums, high cover

With little or no savings element, the same premium buys more cover.

Built up with riders

Critical illness, hospital and accident riders can be added to life cover to suit what you need.

04Risks and points to note

  • Higher premiums on renewalOn renewable plans, premiums rise as you get older. Work out the total you will pay over the long term in advance.
  • Different policy definitionsDefinitions of critical illness, waiting periods and exclusions vary by company and plan. Products with the same name can pay out in different situations.
  • Little or no surrender valueProtection-focused products return little or nothing if you cancel.
  • Claiming from abroadClaims follow the Hong Kong insurer's procedures. Check in advance how Korean medical certificates and records must be translated and what the insurer will accept.

05Checkpoints for Korean residents

  • Limits on offering and arranging it in KoreaBeing offered this product in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary, is restricted in principle.Korea's Insurance Business Act, Article 3
  • Overlap with Korean insuranceFirst compare whether the cover overlaps with Korean indemnity or diagnosis-benefit insurance you already hold. Indemnity-type cover may not pay twice for the same costs.
  • Applying in Hong KongHong Kong insurers usually require you to apply and sign in person locally, and health underwriting follows local standards.
  • Remittance and reportingWhen sending premiums abroad, check the bank's questions about the purpose of the transaction, whether a foreign exchange report is required, and whether you must report the policy as an overseas financial account.National Tax Service · Overseas financial account reporting
