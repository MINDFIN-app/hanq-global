WHY OVERSEAS

Why overseas finance is worth knowing

More than 97% of the world's stock market lies outside Korea.[1] Knowing overseas financial products gives you a yardstick: wider markets, other currencies and the products each country has developed, set against what is on offer at home. Exchange rates, regulation and tax come with them, so we set out the benefits and the risks side by side.

WHY IT MATTERS

Four reasons overseas finance matters

01

Most of the world's market is outside Korea

In the MSCI ACWI IMI, an index covering the world's stock markets (end of June 2026), Korea's weight is 2.8%, against 62.7% for the US, 5.6% for Japan, 3.1% for the UK and 3.0% for Canada.[1] Holding only Korean assets means investing in little more than 3% of the world market.

02

Your assets lean on one currency, the won

If your salary, home and savings are all in won, everything shrinks together when the won weakens. Assets in other currencies, such as the dollar or the yen, help to spread that concentration. Conversely, when the won strengthens, their value in won falls.

03

Each country has developed different products

In Hong Kong, 85.5% of new long-term insurance premiums in 2025 went to participating savings and whole life plans;[2] in Canada, equity ETFs made up 52.1% of ETF net sales in the same year.[3] Because the best-selling structures differ from market to market, they give you something to compare Korean products against.

04

These markets publish their evidence

In all five countries this site covers, regulators and industry associations publish sales statistics, and each country has its own deposit protection scheme. Korean deposit protection, however, does not apply. You can compare products while checking the statistics and the original sources.

BENEFITS

The benefits of overseas financial products

  1. Geographic diversificationSpread your money across several markets rather than relying on one country's economy or stock market.
  2. Currency diversificationHold part of your assets in dollars, yen, pounds and so on, and prepare in advance for money you will spend in a foreign currency, such as for study abroad, emigration or travel.
  3. A wider choice of productsCompare structures that are rare in Korea, such as currency share classes, distributing and accumulating versions, and participating savings plans.
  4. Long-term structuresSome markets have well-developed long-term products, such as savings plans designed to be held for decades.
  5. A benchmark for comparisonKnowing which types sell most overseas gives you a yardstick for the costs and structures of Korean products.
RISKS

What to weigh as heavily as the benefits

  • 1

    Exchange rates

    Returns measured in won change with exchange rates, and currency exchange and transfers cost money.

  • 2

    Rules on selling in Korea

    Being offered overseas insurance in Korea, or buying it through an intermediary there, is restricted in principle,[4] and overseas funds not registered in Korea cannot be offered or sold in Korea.[5]

  • 3

    Tax and reporting

    Interest, dividends and gains earned abroad must also be reported in Korea, and large balances in overseas accounts bring a duty to report them as overseas financial accounts. Tax at a glance →

  • 4

    Different protections

    Korean deposit protection does not apply, and any dispute has to be settled under local law and in the local language.

  • 5

    Possible loss of capital

    Investment products move with market prices with no capital guarantee, and an insurance policy surrendered early can pay back less than the premiums you have paid.
