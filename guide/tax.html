TAX BASICS

Overseas financial products and tax, at a glance

If you live in Korea, you pay tax in Korea on income earned abroad too. Where the country holding the product deducts tax first, the tax treaty sets the maximum, and Korea gives credit for that tax. Rather than detailed calculations, we set out only the basic framework.

Basic rulesBy productHong KongSingaporeUKCanadaJapan
KOREA

Basic rules for Korean residents

01

Tax on worldwide income

Korean residents pay tax in Korea on all their income, wherever it arises.[1] Interest, dividends and gains from overseas financial institutions are also reported by the taxpayer in May of the following year.[1]

02

Financial income not taxed at source in Korea

Interest and dividends of up to KRW 20 million a year are normally settled by withholding in Korea, but income received abroad and not withheld in Korea must, whatever the amount, be added to your other income and reported for comprehensive income tax (progressive rates of 6–45%).[1]

03

Credit for tax paid abroad

Tax already deducted in the country holding the product is subtracted from Korean tax, up to a limit (the foreign tax credit).[1] It stops the same income being taxed twice.

04

Reporting overseas financial accounts

If the combined balance of your accounts with overseas financial institutions exceeds KRW 500 million on the last day of any month, you report them in June of the following year. Failing to report, or reporting too little, can bring a fine of up to 20% of the amount.[4] Under its tax treaties, Korea regularly exchanges financial information with partner countries.[4]

05

Inheritance and gifts

Overseas financial assets are included in the estate a resident leaves and in the gifts a resident receives.[5]

TREATIES

Tax treaties, country by country

A tax treaty caps the tax that the country holding the product can deduct from a Korean resident, and Korea credits that tax so that the same income is not taxed twice. The caps below are for individual investors; the tax actually deducted may be lower, or there may be none, under local law.

Hong Kong

Tax treaty
Korea–Hong Kong tax agreement — signed July 2014, in force September 2016[6]
Dividends and interest
Hong Kong does not withhold tax on dividends or interest.[11] The treaty caps are 15% on dividends and 10% on interest.[6]
Gains on sale
The treaty leaves Hong Kong free to tax them as well,[6] but Hong Kong has no capital gains tax.[11] For Hong Kong products, then, the tax you pay is mainly Korean.

Singapore

Tax treaty
Korea–Singapore tax agreement — signed May 2019, in force December 2019 (replacing the 1979 agreement)[7]
Dividends and interest
Singapore does not tax dividends from Singapore companies (one-tier system), unit trust distributions or interest on deposits with approved banks.[12] Other interest subject to withholding is capped at 10% under the treaty.[7]
Gains on sale
Under the treaty, ordinary investors' gains on shares are taxed only in Korea (with exceptions such as holdings of 25% or more).[7]

UK

Tax treaty
Korea–UK tax convention — signed October 1996, in force December 1996[8]
Dividends and interest
For non-residents, UK tax on UK bank interest and UK company dividends is limited to any tax deducted at source.[13] The treaty caps are 15% on dividends and 10% on interest.[8]
Gains on sale
Non-residents do not pay UK Capital Gains Tax when they sell shares in UK companies (except UK property and property-rich companies).[14] Under the treaty, too, the gains are taxed only in Korea.[8]

Canada

Tax treaty
Korea–Canada tax convention — signed September 2006, in force December 2006[9]
Dividends and interest
Canada withholds 25% on dividends paid to non-residents, reduced to 15% under the treaty.[15][9] Interest paid by an arm's-length payer is not subject to withholding.[15]
Gains on sale
Under the treaty, ordinary investors' gains on shares are taxed only in Korea (with exceptions such as holdings of 25% or more).[9]

Japan

Tax treaty
Korea–Japan tax convention — signed October 1998, in force November 1999[10]
Dividends and interest
Japan withholds 15.315% (including the special reconstruction income tax) on non-residents' dividends from listed shares, distributions from publicly offered investment trusts, and interest.[16] The treaty caps are 15% on dividends and 10% on interest; to claim them, you must submit a tax treaty application form through the local financial institution before the first payment.[10][17]
Gains on sale
Under the treaty, ordinary investors' gains on shares are taxed only in Korea (with exceptions such as large disposals by holders of 25% or more).[10]
